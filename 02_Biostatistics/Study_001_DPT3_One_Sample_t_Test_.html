1. Background

Diphtheria, pertussis, and tetanus (DPT) remain vaccine-preventable diseases of major public health importance, particularly among children under five years of age. Achieving and sustaining high coverage with the third dose of the DPT-containing vaccine (DPT3) is widely used as a key indicator of the performance of routine immunization programmes.

The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends that countries achieve at least 90% national DPT3 coverage to provide adequate population-level protection and reduce the risk of outbreaks of vaccine-preventable diseases. Failure to attain this benchmark may indicate gaps in vaccine delivery, inequitable access to immunization services, or weaknesses in health system performance.

Despite ongoing efforts to strengthen routine immunization in Nigeria, substantial variation in DPT3 coverage persists across states. Understanding whether the observed average state-level coverage differs from the WHO-recommended target is important for evaluating programme performance and informing national immunization strategies.

This study uses state-level DPT3 coverage data to assess whether Nigeria’s mean DPT3 coverage is statistically consistent with the WHO target of 90%.

2. Epidemiologic Question

Is the average DPT3 vaccination coverage across Nigerian states statistically different from the WHO-recommended national target of 90%?

3. Objective

To evaluate whether the mean DPT3 vaccination coverage across Nigerian states is statistically different from the WHO-recommended national coverage target of 90%.

4. Statistical method

Method: One-Sample t-test

Rationale: A one-sample t-test is appropriate because the analysis compares the mean DPT3 coverage from a single sample of Nigerian states with a fixed reference value (the WHO target of 90%).

5. Reference value

The reference value for this analysis is the World Health Organization (WHO) recommended DPT3 immunization coverage target of 90%, which is considered the minimum coverage required to achieve population-level (herd) immunity against diphtheria, pertussis, and tetanus. This benchmark serves as the hypothesized population mean against which the observed state-level DPT3 coverage is statistically evaluated.

6. Data source

State-level DPT3 vaccination coverage data were compiled from published secondary sources.

Three state-specific coverage estimates—Ebonyi (98.7%), Sokoto (11.5%), and the Federal Capital Territory (79.6%)—were obtained directly from Olulaja & Jaber (2026, Public Health Challenges) and independently corroborated using Tribune Online.

The published report also confirmed that the following seven states achieved ≥80% DPT3 coverage:

  • Ebonyi, Enugu, Imo, Ekiti, Lagos, Edo, Osun

and that the following seven states recorded <50% coverage:

  • Sokoto, Zamfara, Borno, Bauchi, Gombe, Niger, Katsina

Because complete state-specific coverage estimates for the remaining 23 states were not publicly available, their values were synthetically generated within the reported coverage bands while preserving the published national mean of approximately 56%. The resulting dataset therefore satisfies all published constraints but does not represent official surveillance records.

Actual district (LGA)-level DPT3 coverage data are maintained within the Nigerian District Health Information System 2 (DHIS2) and are not publicly accessible.

Accordingly, this dataset should be regarded as a synthetic dataset calibrated to verified published statistics and is intended solely to demonstrate epidemiologic and statistical methodology. Findings should therefore be interpreted as illustrative of analytical methods rather than official programmatic estimates or surveillance conclusions.

A one-sample t-test was conducted to determine whether the mean DPT3 vaccination coverage across Nigerian states differed significantly from the WHO-recommended target of 90%.

The analysis showed that the mean DPT3 coverage (55.1%) was significantly lower than the WHO target of 90% (t(36) = −8.09, p = 1.304 × 10⁻⁹).

The 95% confidence interval for the population mean DPT3 coverage ranged from 46.35% to 63.85%, which does not include the WHO target of 90%.

Therefore, the null hypothesis was rejected. There is sufficient statistical evidence to conclude that the mean DPT3 vaccination coverage across Nigerian states is significantly different from the WHO-recommended target of 90%.


A one-sample t-test indicated that mean DPT3 coverage across Nigerian states (M = 55.1%, SD = 26.25) was significantly lower than the WHO-recommended threshold of 90%, t(36) = −8.09, p < .001, 95% CI [46.35, 63.85], d = −1.33. This represents a large effect, suggesting that routine childhood immunization coverage remains inadequate in many Nigerian states, and, on average, states fall substantially short of the coverage needed to sustain population-level immunity against diphtheria, pertussis, and tetanus.
